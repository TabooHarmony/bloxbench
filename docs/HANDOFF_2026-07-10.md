## what BloxBench is

BloxBench is a construction benchmark and failure microscope. It is not currently a proven construction agent, product, or general spatial-reasoning solution.

Keep these layers separate:

- benchmark: fixed Roblox construction and repair tasks
- harness: orchestration, tool loop, screenshots, traces, structure dumps, and experimental modes
- model: the source of actual 3d construction capability
- product: a possible future asset-first Roblox building workflow

The recent work mostly tested harness interventions. It did not establish a reliable improvement in underlying model capability.

## major evidence

### rejected or non-promoted interventions

- PartPrimitives and helper abstractions increased complexity and did not produce a reliable visual win.
- decomposition/protocol guidance produced a failed clean watchtower replicate.
- strict spatial tolerances increased churn and exceeded the token cap.
- relation-oracle tooling was not adopted, exceeded the cap, and regressed visually.
- automatic post-edit spatial feedback reduced rounds and tokens but moved the roof into the doorway and damaged the lookout assembly.
- the MiniMax comparison inspected the scene but made zero edits, so it is not model-quality evidence.

### relation-grounded representation follow-up

The relation-context pilot kept raw Luau and the Roblox tool surface unchanged. On the original two-task set, raw and relation both passed `2/2`, but the offline declared relation audit scored raw `1/2` and relation `2/2`. The relation watchtower used 29 parts versus raw's 57 and reduced output tokens and latency.

A structured pre-edit commitment was then tested with the relation context. It passed `2/2` and produced a restrained 10-part watchtower with the named foundation, shaft, door, platform, column, roof, and four battlements. It also changed the existing platform material and rebuilt the upper rim, so this is not yet preservation evidence. On a third matched roof-attachment layout, raw, relation, and commitment all passed `1/1` and produced the same 16-part artifact. Relation reduced input/output/latency; commitment increased cost without improving the artifact.

The relation representation is promising research infrastructure, not a promoted default. The commitment is conditional and should not be enabled by default. Read `docs/experiments/spatial_relation_followup.md` for exact metrics, strict audit rules, and artifacts.

## current conclusion

The research does not support adding another Spatial mechanism.

What has been learned:

- current agents can perform some local edits
- they struggle with multi-part composition and persistent 3d attachment semantics
- interactive harness loops increase cost and edit churn
- compile-once reduces churn but does not solve the capability gap
- explicit contracts reduce optional overbuilding but do not guarantee geometry relations
- generic structural metrics produce false positives and require human screenshot review
- the benchmark has had fixture and gate bugs, so its labels are not yet strong enough for training or preference data

## recommended strategic pause

Do not immediately jump to model/data. That is a separate program and requires trustworthy labels.

The strongest next angle is benchmark stabilization as a capability microscope:

1. freeze actor/verifier, spatial feedback, compile-once, and contract modes as historical arms
2. create a small clean repair ladder:
   - one-part repair
   - two-defect repair
   - preservation-sensitive assembly repair
3. define required features and allowed changes per task
4. use independent checks only for those requirements
5. separate artifact quality from efficiency and tool behavior
6. run current vanilla on the ladder before changing the agent again
7. use the resulting failure taxonomy to decide whether model/data or routing is justified

If the larger reviewer believes the immediate product goal matters more than research, it should recommend an explicit split: keep BloxBench as a regression microscope and pursue an asset-first Roblox workflow separately.

## required review output

The larger-context reviewer should return:

- whether BloxBench is currently trustworthy enough for model comparison
- whether benchmark stabilization or model/data is the correct next layer
- whether the asset-first product path should split now
- one concrete bounded next action
- explicit stop criteria
- what not to touch

It should inspect the source and pulled artifacts, not rely on stale summaries or `--no-gate PASS`.
